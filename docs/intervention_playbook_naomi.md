# Intervention Playbook: Naomi

This playbook outlines forward-focused constructive feedback and intervention strategies for Naomi. It draws upon the Two-Lane Repair Protocol and grounds the feedback in verified corpus incidents.

---

## 1. The Two-Lane Repair Protocol (Naomi's Grievance Lane)

When Naomi is addressing harm caused by Alex, the goal is to give her a structured path to seek accountability without collapsing into relentless questioning, assuming malice, or escalating into global blame.

### Constructive Grievance Behaviors (What to do)
1. **Name the incident specifically:**
   - *"I want to talk about one specific event where I felt hurt."*
2. **State impact without accusation inflation:**
   - *"When X happened, I felt dismissed / unsafe / unseen."*
   - *"The part that affected me most was Y."*
3. **Request accountability clearly (The Four Factors):**
   - *Apology request:* "I need clear acknowledgment of what happened without minimising it."
   - *Restorative request:* "I need to know what will be different next time."
   - *Caring request:* "I need signs that my pain matters to you."
   - *Anti-diversion request:* "I need us not to switch immediately into my faults while I am trying to explain this."
4. **Avoid global identity claims:**
   - *Prefer:* "In this incident I felt victimized."
   - *Avoid:* "You are a coward / a liar."

### Prohibited Moves (Diverting Strategies)
- Flooding the channel with rapid-fire rhetorical questions.
- Assuming the worst possible intent behind clumsy communication.
- Retaliatory character attacks when boundaries are drawn.

## 2. Case Study: The "Keyboard Warrior" Incident (CONF-02)

**Context:** The incident was catalyzed by Alex's potential move into Nishant's house. Alex awkwardly attempted to set a boundary regarding "sexual tension," catching Naomi completely off guard in person. 

### Constructive Criticism & Accountability

**1. Assigning Malice to Incompetence**
Instead of seeing Alex as clumsy or poor at emotional communication, Naomi immediately ascribed malicious intent to him. She accused him of "acting dumb," "playing the fool," and intentionally leading her on to "feel wanted." 
* **The Accountability:** Naomi needs to own that she did not give Alex the benefit of the doubt. She assumed that his awkwardness was a deliberate psychological manipulation, turning a clumsy boundary-setting attempt into an intentional attack.

**2. The Interrogation Tactic**
Once she felt safe behind her screen and sent her asynchronous voice notes, she flooded him with intense questions and demanded he answer them immediately. When Alex (who actively avoids heavy emotional conflict) deflected, she used it as proof of his poor character ("coward").
* **The Accountability:** Naomi needs to take accountability for weaponizing her emotional vocabulary. Just because she can process and articulate complex emotions rapidly does not mean Alex can. Pushing someone who is already retreating only guarantees a fight.

**3. Refusal to Accept the "No"**
Ultimately, Alex was trying to communicate a boundary: he only wanted to be friends. Instead of grieving the rejection, Naomi turned it into a cross-examination of his entire character (culminating in calling him a "chaser"). 
* **The Accountability:** Naomi needs to own that she retaliated against the rejection by trying to emotionally destroy him. Accepting a "no" gracefully, even when delivered poorly, is a necessary boundary-respecting skill.
