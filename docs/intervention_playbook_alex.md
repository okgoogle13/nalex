# Intervention Playbook: Alex

This playbook outlines forward-focused constructive feedback and intervention strategies for Alex. It draws upon the Two-Lane Repair Protocol and grounds the feedback in verified corpus incidents.

---

## 1. The Two-Lane Repair Protocol (Alex's Accountability Lane)

When Alex is the one who caused harm or triggered a conflict (even unintentionally), the goal is to keep him out of defensive diversion and move him into constructive repair behaviors.

### Constructive Repair Behaviors (What to do)
1. **Name the lane:**
   - *"In this conversation, I am focusing on what I did and how it affected you."*
2. **Deliver an apology with responsibility, not self-protection:**
   - *"I did X."*
   - *"I can see it hurt you in Y way."*
   - *"I take responsibility for that."*
3. **Offer restorative action:**
   - *"What I will change is Z."*
4. **Reserve personal grievances for a later lane:**
   - *"I also have my own hurt, and I want us to address that separately so it doesn’t take away from repairing this moment first."*

### Prohibited Moves (Diverting Strategies)
- *"You made me do it."*
- *"You’re overreacting."*
- *"That’s not what happened."*
- *"You do the same thing to me."*
- Minimizing the other person's reality (e.g., *"It's not that complicated"*).

## 2. Case Study: The "Keyboard Warrior" Incident (CONF-02)

**Context:** The incident was catalyzed by Alex's potential move into Nishant's house. Alex wanted to preemptively clear the air about any "sexual tension" with Naomi to avoid awkwardness, but forced the conversation on his timeline in person.

### Constructive Criticism & Accountability

**1. Misreading "Freeze" as Consent**
When Alex asked Naomi if she was okay in person, she shut down and stared at her feet because she was flooded. Alex interpreted this silence as compliance or withholding, rather than a trauma/overwhelm response. 
* **The Accountability:** Alex needs to own that he forced a heavy, boundary-setting conversation on his timeline, failed to read the room, and offered zero consideration for how jarring the news would be for her.

**2. The "Keyboard Warrior" Invalidation**
When Naomi finally processed her feelings and communicated them asynchronously via voice note from a safe distance, Alex immediately attacked her medium of communication ("the second I leave, you're a fucking keyboard warrior").
* **The Accountability:** By attacking *how* she communicated, he entirely invalidated *what* she was saying. He needs to take accountability for using a cheap shot to shut down a conversation he didn't want to have.

**3. Minimization and Escalation**
When Naomi expressed feeling blindsided ("had the rug pulled out"), Alex minimized her reality (*"It's not that complicated"*). When she pushed back, he escalated to calling her a "narcissist" and accusing her of using ChatGPT.
* **The Accountability:** Alex needs to acknowledge that his delivery was blunt, and that his immediate pivot to defensiveness and name-calling poured gasoline on the fire. He needs to validate that it is okay for Naomi to be hurt by his boundary, even if the boundary itself was valid.
